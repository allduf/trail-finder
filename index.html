    How Trail Finder works

    All data comes live from OpenStreetMap. By default it covers the Sea to Sky corridor and the Sunshine Coast, split into the zones in the Area menu. You can also search any other place, or pan the map and tap “Search this area”. It finds paths and tracks that were first added to the map inside the period you pick.

    Trail name search

    The search box looks through every named path, track, bike path and trail route in the whole region, old or new, plus the new trails already found. Typos and close spellings still match. The first time you use it, it downloads the list of names area by area (closest area first) and saves it in this browser for a week. Pick a result to zoom to the trail and see everything known about it.

    What counts as new

    A way is new when its first version is dated inside the period. Overpass gives every way edited in the last 2 years. For ways with more than one version, the app asks the OpenStreetMap API for version 1 and uses its date. A trail that was only edited (retagged, extended) does not count.

    One catch: when a mapper splits an old trail into two pieces, the new piece gets a new id and shows up as new. Check the history link in the popup when something looks off.

    MTB tagged

    Any tag starting with mtb (mtb:scale, mtb:scale:imba, mtb:type, mtb=yes, …) or membership in a route=mtb relation. mtb=no is excluded.

    Looks like MTB

    A path, bridleway or rough track (grade 3 to 5) with no MTB tags that scores 2 points or more:

    • +2 bikes allowed: bicycle=yes, designated or permissive
    • +1 natural surface: dirt, earth, ground, mud, rock, roots, woodchips
    • +1 rough: smoothness bad or worse
    • +1 narrow: width under 1.5 m
    • +1 trail_visibility or incline is mapped
    • +1 the name sounds like a bike trail (flow, berm, jump, enduro, downhill, singletrack, piste, sentier…)
    • +1 a track graded grade4 or grade5

    Skipped: paved surfaces, bicycle=dismount, and alpine hiking routes (sac_scale alpine).

    Unofficial (possibly unsanctioned) trails

    Marked with a dark dotted line and an “Unofficial?” tag. A trail is flagged when mappers tagged it informal=yes, closed it (access=no or private), banned bikes (bicycle=no, mtb=no), or wrote “unsanctioned”, “closed”, “illegal” or similar in its note or description. Turn on “Unofficial only” to see just those. This is a hint from the map data, not the legal status: many unsanctioned trails aren't tagged at all, and some tags are out of date.

    Other paths

    New highway=path ways that don't reach 2 points. Many fresh trails are mapped with no extra tags, so this layer is worth a look.
