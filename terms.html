Last updated November 4, 2026

Before your first route or tour of each kind, the app asks you to tap I understand and accept on a short version of this.

Trails, roads and land

  • Maps come from OpenStreetMap, governments, clubs, resorts and riders. They can be wrong, old or missing things.
  • A trail on the map isn't permission to use it. Trails may be closed, private, on First Nations land, in a park with its own rules, or not built for bikes. Unofficial trails are shown as plain paths and may not be allowed. Follow signs and land managers.
  • Difficulty, loam, snow, weather and trail reports are estimates. Conditions change fast.

Avalanches and winter travel

Avalanche danger, slope shading and route checks come from Avalanche Canada and from our own reading of the terrain. They don't replace avalanche training, the official forecast at avalanche.ca, your own judgment on the slope, or the gear (transceiver, probe, shovel) and a partner. Slackcountry and backcountry areas are outside ski patrol.

Hunting and fishing rules

Seasons, zones, limits, no-shooting areas and fishing rules in the app are short summaries to help you plan. The official regulations of your province or territory are what count: read them, and check for closures and changes before you go. Crown land and property lines are approximate. Ask before going on private land.

Navigation and live location

Don't rely only on your phone: batteries die, GPS drifts and there may be no signal. Live location and late alerts need signal and can fail. They aren't a rescue service. In an emergency, call 911.

AI answers

Photo find, Ask in plain words and fish ID use Google's AI (only if you say yes, 18 or older). AI answers can be wrong. Check a fish's species and the rules before you keep it.

What you post

When you post a trail report, a grade, a photo, a rider-added trail or a shared ride:

  • It must be true, about the trail, and yours to share. No hate, harassment, threats, nudity, spam, ads, or anything illegal. Don't post other people's faces or private details without their OK, or directions onto private land.
  • You keep it, but you let Trail Finder show it to other users in the app, and use it to make the app better (for example, to fix trail data).
  • Anyone can tap ⚑ Report on a post or Hide this rider. Posts several riders report are hidden until checked. We can remove any post or close any account that breaks these rules, without notice.
  • You can delete your posts, or your whole account, any time (More → Account → Settings → Delete my account).

Fair use

Don't copy the app's data in bulk, try to break or overload it, or get around its daily limits. Free services have limits; some features may stop for the day when they're reached.

Other people's content

Maps, photos, webcams and data belong to their owners (see How it works → Credits). Links to other sites are for your convenience; we don't control them.

No warranty

The app is given as it is, with no promise that it's accurate, complete or always working. To the extent the law allows, Trail Finder and the people who make it aren't responsible for any accident, injury, illness, death, rescue cost, or loss of or damage to property, or any other loss, from using the app or relying on it, its routes or other users' posts, even if we made a mistake. If we are found responsible anyway, the most we owe is CA$100. You agree to cover our reasonable costs if someone makes a claim against us because you broke these terms or the law. Some places, including Quebec, don't allow some of these limits, so they apply only as far as the law there allows.

Sentiers, chemins et terrains

Les sentiers non officiels sont montrés comme de simples chemins et peuvent être interdits. Suivez les panneaux et les gestionnaires du territoire.
