Trails, roads and land

  • Maps come from OpenStreetMap, governments, clubs, resorts and riders. They can be wrong, old or missing things.
  • A trail on the map isn't permission to use it. Trails may be closed, private, on First Nations land, in a park with its own rules, or not built for bikes. Unofficial trails are shown as plain paths and may not be allowed. Follow signs and land managers.
  • Difficulty, loam, snow, weather and trail reports are estimates. Conditions change fast.

Avalanches and winter travel

Avalanche danger, slope shading and route checks come from Avalanche Canada (and the Japan Avalanche Network in Japan) and from our own reading of the terrain. They don't replace avalanche training, the official forecast at avalanche.ca, your own judgment on the slope, or the gear (transceiver, probe, shovel) and a partner. Slackcountry and backcountry areas are outside ski patrol.

Backcountry routes and avalanche tools

Every backcountry tool in the app is a computer's estimate to help you plan. None of them tells you a slope or a route is safe, and none of them makes the decision for you.

  • Safest way and Plan a tour. The app picks a line by weighing slope angle, aspect, time spent on or under steep slopes, terrain traps, the regional danger rating and the weather. How much each one counts is our judgment from avalanche course rules of thumb, not measured odds. Its steps, Why this way, Watch out, One at a time, Don't stop and places to regroup come from the same model.
  • Terrain model. It works from a 10 to 30 m elevation map. It can't see cornices, wind loading, small slopes, cliffs, crevasses, tree density, open creeks or today's snowpack.
  • ATES terrain classes. Where a park or Avalanche Canada publishes an official rating, the app shows it as we copied it, and it can be out of date. Everywhere else the class is our own estimate, not an official rating.
  • Danger ratings and trip-planner lights (Normal, Extra caution, Not recommended) follow Avalanche Canada's forecast and Avaluator chart. A forecast covers a whole region and can be late or missing. The light is for planning, not a go or no-go decision on the day.
  • Weather, new snow, wind, rain and warming come from weather models (Open-Meteo and others) and can be wrong, especially in the mountains.
  • Official status (Rogers Pass restricted areas, gondola and lift status, road and trail closures, park notices) is copied from the people who run them and can be late or wrong. Their own sign or website is what counts.
  • GPS tracks, skin tracks, heatmaps and other people's trips show where someone went once. They don't mean it was safe then, or that it's safe now.
  • Alerts (avalanche danger, watched tours, late-trip alerts) can arrive late or not at all. No alert doesn't mean nothing changed.

Hunting and fishing rules

Seasons, zones, limits, no-shooting areas and fishing rules in the app are short summaries to help you plan. The official regulations of your province or territory are what count: read them, and check for closures and changes before you go. Crown land and property lines are approximate. Ask before going on private land.

Navigation and live location

Don't rely only on your phone: batteries die, GPS drifts and there may be no signal. Live location and late alerts need signal and can fail. They aren't a rescue service. In an emergency, call 911.

AI answers

Photo find, Ask in plain words and fish ID use Google's AI (only if you say yes, 18 or older). AI answers can be wrong. Check a fish's species and the rules before you keep it.

  • Anyone can tap ⚑ Report on a post or Hide this rider. Posts several riders report are hidden until checked. We can remove any post or close any account that breaks these rules, without notice.
  • You can delete your posts, or your whole account, any time (More → Account → Settings → Delete my account).

You choose to do them knowing that, and you accept all those risks.
